The boards

Each board is modelled down to the registers its Linux drivers touch: interrupt controller, timers, storage, display, USB, network and, on most of them, the GPU. The goal everywhere is the stock image, booted unmodified.

Four CPU cores, written from scratch

The boards share their cores, the snapshot format, the virtual network and this site. A new board is mostly its SoC: the devices between the CPU and the connectors.

64-bit Arm

arm-a64

Raspberry Pi 4, Orange Pi 5. ARMv8-A with FP and NEON, exact to Arm’s rounding and NaN rules. A block JIT to AArch64 natively, and to WebAssembly in the browser.

32-bit Arm

arm-a32

Orange Pi One, Tinker Board S, and 32-bit programs on the Pi 4. A32, Thumb-2, VFPv4 and NEON, with JITs to AArch64 and WebAssembly.

64-bit RISC-V

riscv64

Orange Pi RV. RV64GC with the U74’s bit-manipulation extensions and Sv39 paging, under real OpenSBI.

32-bit RISC-V

riscv-rv32

ESP32-S31. RV32IMAC with machine, supervisor and user modes and Sv32 paging, from the esp32sim project.

The demos run on your computer: the emulator is WebAssembly in a Web Worker, and no server runs the boards. sbc-emu is open source under the BSD 3-Clause licence: source on GitHub. What runs in the demos, and its licences.
