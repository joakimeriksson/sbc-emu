3D on an emulated Raspberry Pi 4

Raspberry Pi OS draws these demos with Mesa’s own V3D driver, on a model of the Pi 4’s VideoCore VI GPU: its binner, rasterizer and shader cores, compiled to WebAssembly and running on your computer.

    Inside the emulated BCM2711

    Every block below, the GPU included, is modelled in Rust, compiled to WebAssembly for this page, and driven by the unmodified Linux drivers.

    4 × Cortex-A72

    ARMv8-A cores with FP and NEON, an MMU and timers. A block JIT translates guest code to WebAssembly.

    V3D 4.2 GPU

    The binner, rasterizer, tile buffer, texture unit and QPU shader cores. Mesa's OpenGL ES 3.1 and Vulkan drivers run on it.

    HVS, pixel valve, HDMI

    The vc4 display pipeline: planes, vblank, EDID, HDMI audio.

    PCIe, VL805 xHCI

    USB keyboard, tablet, stick and webcam on the USB-A ports.

    GENET, CYW43455

    Ethernet and Wi-Fi with the stock drivers, behind a virtual network.

    GPIO, I²C, SPI, PCM

    An LED, a button, a BME280, an MCP3008 and an I²S DAC.

    sbc-emu runs entirely in this tab: the emulator is WebAssembly in a Web Worker. This page has no SD card: the snapshot was taken with the demos’ programs, Mesa and their shaders already in the guest’s memory, so commands other than the demos may fail.

    The 3D demos are kmscube and scenes from glmark2, from the Raspberry Pi OS and Debian archives (versions, licences and sources). Frame rates are what the emulated GPU manages on your computer, not a real Pi 4.
