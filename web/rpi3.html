sbc-emu Raspberry Pi 3

Broadcom BCM2837, four 64-bit Cortex-A53s, emulated instruction by instruction in your browser
Board

Your own SD card

Drop a 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS card image here, as you downloaded it (.img.xz, .img.gz or .img), or choose it. It stays on your computer: the emulator reads it where it is, and what the Pi writes is kept in memory until you close the tab.

  • Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit) from raspberrypi.com (0.5 GB). The page unpacks it once into your browser’s storage (3 GB, about a minute) and reuses that on your next visit. The firmware’s part is emulated: it reads config.txt with the board’s sections, the kernel and its initramfs from the card. On first boot the screen asks for your keyboard layout, then a user name and password: click the screen and type.
  • 64-bit Lite images, not desktops: the Pi here runs at a fraction of a real one’s speed. 32-bit images (kernel7.img) do not boot.

choose a board, then a system to boot

Click the screen to type on the Pi’s USB keyboard (the DWC2 USB host, behind the LAN7515’s or LAN9514’s hub on the B models). The screen is the HDMI monitor: Raspberry Pi OS drives it with the stock vc4 KMS driver, the quick demo with the firmware’s framebuffer. Sound played on HDMI comes out here. The A53s’ code is translated to WebAssembly as it runs (?jit=0 interprets it).
